# 01 — Organization and regulatory scope

**Fictional company:** Aurora Payment Services (APS), imagined EU payment institution with a payment API, customer data systems, outsourced hosting and operations support. All suppliers, systems, regulators, people and data are synthetic.

**DORA:** The entity is *assumed* to fall under the financial entity scope of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 for this educational exercise. Applicability can depend on license/category and exclusions. A payment API is treated as supporting a critical or important function, but this is a scenario assumption rather than an authoritative classification. The competent authority and required jurisdiction-specific technical submission mechanics must be verified in an actual assessment.

**GDPR:** APS is hypothetically a controller for identifiable customer information. Some ICT vendors may be processors for particular processing activities; this is not inferred automatically for every service. There are no real personal data records in the project.

## Distinction from the two other portfolio projects

The supplier-risk project evaluates security controls and onboarding choices. The ISO 27001 project maps internal ISMS requirements and improvement gaps. This project instead models **regulatory assessment, reporting decisions and traceability**, with deliberately separate DORA and GDPR incidents, clocks, evidence and approval flows.

## In-scope analysis

- Three fictional incidents with service impact and privacy attributes.
- DORA classification and reporting prerequisites under the detailed incident RTS.
- GDPR Articles 33–34 notification criteria and controller/processor distinction.
- High-level DORA ICT risk, resilience and ICT arrangement obligations.
- Synthetic evidence and remediation owner assignments.

## Out of scope

Real legal advice, filing to authorities, personal data processing, external assurance, regulatory compliance certification, official DORA register of information submission, exact ITS format reproduction, and assessment of every delegated/implementing regulation. The dataset covers a **selected set of 19 regulatory checkpoints**, not comprehensive legal compliance.
